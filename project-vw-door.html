Multi-Axis Door — Volkswagen

Stanford Mechanical Engineering senior capstone in partnership with the Volkswagen Innovation and Engineering Center California (IECC). Instead of swinging on a hinge, the door translates outward and then forward along the body, clearing the entire aperture so drivers with reduced mobility can get in and out without fighting a door swing or a tight parking space.

The Problem

A conventional hinged door sweeps an arc beside the car. For drivers with reduced mobility, that arc is the problem: the open door blocks the path to the seat, needs a lot of side clearance, and forces people to twist around it. Aftermarket fixes such as ramps and lifts add cost and complexity and are hard to build into the vehicle at the factory.

The Design

The door moves on two axes. First, carriages on guide rails at the sill and header push it straight out, clearing the body. Then it slides forward, parallel to the vehicle, leaving the whole aperture open with nothing sticking out into the entry path. Because the door moves in straight lines instead of an arc, it needs much less side clearance and opens up far more usable entrance volume.

  • Outward-then-forward translation replaces the hinge arc
  • Upper and lower guide rails keep the door parallel to the body throughout travel
  • Opening designed around a seated occupant's ingress and egress path

Process

We started with user research on how people with reduced mobility get in and out of vehicles, turned those findings into requirements, and iterated from concept mechanisms to a validated full-scale prototype of a working mechanical system, with guidance from Volkswagen IECC engineers throughout.
