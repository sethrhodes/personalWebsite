Spider Egg — Team Hydra Strike

Submarines run out of torpedoes long before they run out of targets. As Team Hydra Strike in Stanford's Hacking for Defense, sponsored by the Naval Undersea Warfare Center (NUWC) Newport, we set out to help the Navy "Sink Smarter, Not Harder." The result is Spider Egg: a modular, autonomous UUV that launches from existing submarine torpedo tubes and carries mission-specific payloads, including kinetic "Spiders," FPV drones, sensors, and EW and relay modules. One tube becomes many effects. We took it from a classroom problem statement to a working prototype, outside funding, connections with the Office of Naval Research, and briefings with Navy leadership and Congress.

The Problem

NUWC Newport brought our team a blunt challenge: the Navy's undersea strike capacity still relies on the Mk 48 heavyweight torpedo, one weapon for one target. Our research found that a submarine can carry only about 25 torpedoes before it needs a 3–4 day resupply. In a high-tempo conflict, that magazine depth is a strategic liability.

Our Answer

Spider Egg is a torpedo-sized, modular UUV that fits existing submarine tubes and acts as a carrier rather than a single warhead. Near the target it releases smaller kinetic effectors (the "Spiders") for precise mission kills or coordinated hard kills. It can also launch FPV drones, surface a thermal/EO and comms mast, or deploy sensors and relays. Because the architecture is open and platform-agnostic, the same vehicle works with submarines, surface combatants, and large UUVs.

  • More effects per tube: one launch delivers up to three Spiders plus a deck of drones or sensors
  • Scalable lethality: disable propulsion or sensors, or converge for a decisive hard kill
  • Multi-domain reach: undersea, surface, and air effects from a single vehicle
  • Fits today's fleet: built to launch from existing tubes, so no new platform is needed

The Program

Spider Egg began in Stanford's Hacking for Defense in Spring 2025, where Team Hydra Strike took on NUWC Newport's problem and ran 110+ stakeholder interviews in a single quarter, including Navy commanders and submarine weapons officers. Through the Defense Innovation Unit's Commercialization Fellowship, we then built and tested a 6-foot autonomous UUV prototype in under 8 weeks with just two engineers, secured $80K in non-dilutive funding, and took discovery to 155+ interviews across the DoD.

Capabilities

  • Quiet electric propulsion: a high-energy-density lithium-ion battery (265 kWh) and a low-noise electric motor keep the acoustic signature minimal at speeds up to 30 knots
  • Mission-flexible lower payload bay: holds up to three Spiders, sensor pods, or smaller specialized UUVs
  • Versatile upper payload bay: FPV drones, electronic warfare modules, and buoyant relay sensors
  • Thermal imaging & PACE comms: a mast raises thermal/EO sensors plus SATCOM, Iridium, HF, and VHF links on surfacing
  • Autonomous distributed strikes: directs Spiders individually or couples them to concentrate effects on priority threats
  • Platform-agnostic integration: works with submarine combat systems, surface combatant networks, and large UUVs

The Spider

Each Spider is a compact kinetic effector: 1.5 m long, with quiet electric propulsion, agile fin and rudder control surfaces, and onboard mesh networking for cooperative targeting. It is smaller than a standard Mk 54 torpedo yet built to deliver comparable effects, and it can launch from LWT tubes, large UUVs, beach-launched assets, aerial drones, or a Spider Egg capsule.
